A plumbing visit preparation checklist

A little preparation can make the affected area easier to inspect without requiring you to dismantle anything. Keep a record of what you have noticed, including whether the issue is constant or intermittent. Photos of visible damage can help you explain changes that might not be apparent during the visit.

Before the appointment:

  • Remove stored belongings from around the affected fixture when safe.
  • Keep pets away from the work area.
  • Locate any paperwork for the fixture or appliance involved.
  • Note recent installations, repairs, or changes in water use.
  • Identify the water shutoff location if you already know it.
  • Arrange access with a landlord or building manager when necessary.

Leave plumbing connections intact. Taking apart a fitting before the visit may create another leak or make the original condition harder to assess. If a valve is stuck, avoid forcing it.

Plumbing repair mistakes to avoid

Choosing a repair based only on how a fixture looks can overlook a hidden connection or a recurring drainage problem. Explain previous attempts to fix the issue, including products added to a drain. That history helps the person assessing the plumbing understand what has already changed.

Avoid buying replacement parts before compatibility is established. Faucets, valves, and other components can differ even when their visible shapes look alike. Ask whether an existing part can be repaired and what a replacement would require.

For a shared building, confirm which decisions belong to you and which require management approval. A repair that affects a common supply or drainage line may involve access beyond your unit. Clarifying that responsibility before work begins can prevent confusion.
